## 1962
F20 VERIFIED · Starfish Prime: launched from Johnston Atoll (≈1,450 km WSW of Hawaii) on a Thor rocket; detonated 9 Jul 1962, 09:00:09 UTC (11 pm 8 Jul Honolulu time), 400 km up, 1.4 Mt; 13 min 41 s after liftoff (Wikipedia, US govt reports).
F21 VERIFIED · First attempt 20 Jun 1962 failed; missile destroyed at ~10 km, debris and contamination fell on Johnston Atoll.
F22 VERIFIED · EMP far larger than expected: in Hawaii ~300 streetlights out, burglar alarms, telephone microwave link damaged; Kauai phone calls to other islands cut.
F23 REPORTED · Some Hawaii hotels held "rainbow bomb" rooftop parties (atomic veteran Cecil R. Coale, via Wikipedia).
F24 VERIFIED · Auroras across a huge area of the Pacific, north and south of the equator; seen from Kwajalein 2,600 km away.
F25 VERIFIED · Background: USSR ended 3-year testing moratorium 30 Aug 1961; Starfish part of Operation Fishbowl / Dominic.
F26 VERIFIED · Artificial radiation belt; Ariel, TRAAC and Transit 4B failed; Telstar 1 and others degraded (Wikipedia HANE).
F27 VERIFIED · Telstar 1: launched 10 Jul 1962 (the day after), first live TV US-Europe; failed after ~7 months, from Starfish radiation (Wikipedia). Project cost ~$50 million (AT&T).
F28 VERIFIED · Soviet Project K, 22 Oct 1962 (Cuban Missile Crisis): 300 kt at 290 km over Kazakhstan; EMP fused a 570 km telephone line, burned down the Karaganda power plant, shut 1,000 km of buried power cable (Wikipedia, Russian reports).
F29 VERIFIED · Partial Test Ban Treaty signed 5 Aug 1963 in Moscow, in force 10 Oct 1963; bans tests in atmosphere, space, underwater.
F30 VERIFIED · Effective satellite-kill radius of prompt radiation ≈ 80 km (Wikipedia HANE).

## Why now is worse
F40 ESTIMATE · Petersen, Qin, Thiele, Philippe (Princeton), arXiv 2611.09827, 6 Nov 2026: >13,000 satellites in LEO; 1 Mt at 500 km over the South Atlantic disables ~98% of modelled satellites within one week (central case); tripled radiation tolerance: ~97% within two weeks; ~20 collisions expected within two years; replacements exposed to radiation and debris for years.
F41 REPORTED · Starlink: ~13,096 launched, ~11,193 in orbit (HighSpeedInternet.com, 13 Nov 2026).
F42 REPORTED · SpaceX, Feb 2022: a geomagnetic storm caused up to 40 of 49 newly launched Starlink satellites to re-enter.
F43 VERIFIED · Polar weather satellites (Metop, NOAA) fly at ~850 km (Met Office, Sep 2025). Geostationary at ~35,800 km.
F44 VERIFIED · GPS satellites orbit at ~20,200 km; GPS timing used for traffic signal timing and synchronising mobile phone base stations (Wikipedia GPS).
F45 REPORTED · Ukraine's military and government rely heavily on Starlink (Wikipedia Starlink; Fox 23 Nov 2026).

## The bill
F50 ESTIMATE · London Economics for UK Space Agency/DSIT (pub. 18 Oct 2023): UK GNSS 24-hour outage loss £1,424m; 7-day outage £7,644m; emergency services, maritime and road = 87.6% of the 7-day loss; benefits £13,622m a year.
F51 ESTIMATE · same: emergency services 7-day loss: caller location £1,560m, vehicle and personnel location £1,969m.
F52 ESTIMATE · RTI for NIST (2019): loss of GPS ≈ $1 billion per day to the US; up to 50% higher in April-May planting season; GPS benefits ≈ $1.4 trillion since the 1980s.
F53 ILLUSTRATION · our maths: UK ≈ 28.6m households (ONS 2024): £7,644m / 28.6m ≈ £267 per household for one week; £1,424m ≈ £50 per household per day. US ≈ 132m households (Census 2024): $1bn ≈ $7.60 per household per day, ≈ $53 a week. Not a bill anyone sends; a way to size the loss.
F55 ESTIMATE · London Economics for Innovate UK (2017), 'The economic impact on the UK of a disruption to GNSS': a 5-day outage ≈ £5.2bn. https://londoneconomics.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/LE-IUK-Economic-impact-to-UK-of-a-disruption-to-GNSS-SHOWCASE-PUBLISH-S2C190517.pdf
F54 SCENARIO · a blast in LEO does not switch GPS off by itself; GPS is ~20,000 km up. The DIA says a detonation could be tailored to affect MEO. The UK/US studies price a navigation outage; we use them as the nearest price tags, not a forecast.
