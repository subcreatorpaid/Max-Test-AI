## Description
About 6,000 sailors on up to 400 ships, stuck at sea behind the Strait of Hormuz, by one count. At least 25 dead, by the IMO's. This is the story of the people moving the world's oil through a war zone, why they can't leave, and how the cost of keeping them there reaches your tank, your shopping and your bills.

Since 28 February 2026, war cover for a single trip through Hormuz has risen to 7.5-10% of a ship's value. On one tanker cargo to China, that came to about $21 million. By our estimate that is up to about 25 cents a gallon in the US, or about 6p a litre in the UK, if it is fully passed on. The legal minimum for the sailors carrying that cargo is $690 a month, about $23 a day.

Every number on screen carries a confidence tag (VERIFIED, REPORTED, CLAIM, ESTIMATE) and a source. Counts differ: the IMO said about 20,000 seafarers were stranded in the spring, and DP World said about 6,000 in late October. We show both. This is not financial advice.

If you or someone in your family works at sea and needs help: the International Transport Workers' Federation (itfseafarers.org) and the Mission to Seafarers (missiontoseafarers.org).
