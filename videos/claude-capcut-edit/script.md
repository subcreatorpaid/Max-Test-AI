## Script (~1,450 words, ~10 min at 150 wpm)
`[MAX]` = Max on camera (Flow clip) · `[VO]` = voice over screen · cues in brackets.

### 0:00 Hook
[MAX] (confident) This video was edited by Claude. Not the script. The actual edit.
[VO] [SCREEN: R1 raw timeline, 8 minutes, full of pauses] Here's the raw recording I started with. Eight minutes. Pauses, retakes, me losing my place.
[VO] [SPLIT: raw vs finished, stopwatch overlay] And here's what came out the other side.

### 0:12 Belief challenge
[MAX] I'm Max, and yes, I'm an AI host. Which makes this a strange test: an AI checking whether another AI can do an editor's job.
[VO] [CARD: "AI can write. Can it edit?"] The usual advice says AI is great for scripts and captions, but the timeline is still your problem. You still sit there cutting every "um" by hand.
[VO] [CARD: 3 levels] So I tested Claude at three levels. Level one, Claude plans the edit. Level two, Claude does the boring cuts. Level three, Claude takes the mouse and drives CapCut itself.
[MAX] By the end you'll know which level is worth your time. Level three did not go the way I expected.

### 0:45 Chapter 1: The raw mess
[VO] [SCREEN: R1, zoom on waveform gaps] Here's the footage. It's a normal tutorial recording. You can see every pause in the waveform.
[VO] [SCREEN: R2 CapCut auto captions] Step one is free and built into CapCut. I run auto captions, so the whole video becomes text with timestamps. [CHECK: CapCut desktop free plan includes auto captions and caption export as SRT/TXT]
[VO] [SCREEN: R2 export] Then I export that transcript. This file is how Claude "sees" the video.
[VO] [CARD: "Claude can't watch video. It reads the transcript."] Quick reality check: Claude doesn't watch your footage. It reads the words and the timestamps. That turns out to be enough for most of an edit.

### 1:40 Chapter 2: Level one, Claude plans the edit
[VO] [SCREEN: R3 paste transcript + prompt into Claude] I paste the transcript into Claude with one prompt. I'll put the full prompt in the description.
[VO] [ZOOM: the prompt] I ask for four things. Every section to cut, with timestamps. The best fifteen seconds to use as the hook. Chapter titles. And where to zoom or show the screen.
[VO] [SCREEN: R3 Claude's answer scrolling] [RESULT: how long Claude took and how many cuts it listed]
[VO] [ZOOM: the hook suggestion] And here's the part I didn't expect. [RESULT: something Claude spotted that Max missed, e.g. "it moved my best line from minute six to the opening"]
[MAX] (surprised) That's an editor's instinct. I didn't ask for it to reorder anything.
[VO] [CARD: Level 1 verdict] Level one result: [RESULT: e.g. "a full edit plan in under a minute, about 80% of it usable"]. That alone is worth it.
[VO] But a plan isn't an edit. I still had to do the cutting. So, level two.

### 3:10 Chapter 3: Level two, Claude does the boring cuts
[VO] [SCREEN: R4 CapCut transcript editing] CapCut lets you edit video by deleting text from the transcript. Delete a sentence, and the clip under it disappears. [CHECK: CapCut desktop text-based / transcript editing exists and is free]
[VO] [SCREEN: R4 deleting lines from Claude's list] So I take Claude's cut list and go line by line. Retake, gone. Long pause, gone. Rambling intro, gone.
[VO] [SCREEN: R4 remove pauses tool] CapCut also has a tool to remove pauses automatically. [CHECK: name and availability of CapCut's silence/pause removal tool on the free plan]
[VO] [ZOOM: timeline shrinking, stopwatch] [RESULT: time taken vs the usual manual cut, e.g. "eleven minutes instead of an hour"]
[MAX] Here's the catch though.
[VO] [SCREEN: R4 a bad cut] [RESULT: a real mistake, e.g. "it cut the middle of a sentence because the transcript merged two takes"]. You still need to watch it back. Claude doesn't hear how a cut sounds.
[VO] [CARD: Level 2 verdict] Level two: [RESULT: e.g. "the biggest time saver, with five minutes of checking"].
[VO] Now the real question. Can Claude do this without me clicking anything?

### 5:00 Chapter 4: Level three, Claude takes the mouse
