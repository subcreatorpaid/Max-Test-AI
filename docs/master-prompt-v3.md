# MASTER PROMPT v3: THE INVOICE (geopolitical finance documentaries)

v3, 2026-11-04. It builds on v2 with what we learned making "Round Two". This is the standing master prompt for every Invoice video, used with the production rule in `pipeline/PROCESS.md`.

**Run order:** Stage 0 Topic pick, then Stage A Fact pack, then Stage H Hook lab, then Stage B Script, then Stage V Visual handoff. Hammad OKs three things: the topic (after Stage 0), the script (after Stage B), and the final cut.

---

## STAGE 0: TOPIC SELECTION (run with web search, plus vidIQ when credits allow)

Return 5 candidate topics, each scored 1-5 on:
1. **News peg:** something happened in the last 72 hours, or will happen on a known date within 2 weeks.
2. **Stakes for a normal household:** a clear path to fuel, food, rent, mortgage, jobs or savings.
3. **Search and outlier demand:** related videos from the last 30 days doing more than 3x their channel's average (vidIQ outliers or YouTube search); give 2-3 examples with views.
4. **Gap:** the angle the big channels haven't taken. Old crowded topics with no new stakes flop.
5. **Visual fit:** can we show it with maps, charts, number cards and stock B-roll Hammad can download (ships, ports, refineries, money, supermarkets, petrol stations, trading screens)?
6. **Fact depth:** enough VERIFIED and REPORTED numbers for 20-30 minutes.

For each candidate, give the topic type (A strike/war, B choke point/blockade, C sanctions/money war, D follow-up "round two" of a story we already covered), a working title, a thumbnail promise (one image, 2-3 words, one glowing element) and the total score.

Rules:
- Pick the highest total. A tie goes to the stronger news peg.
- Don't post two big videos on the same story within a week unless the second is a clear "what changed" follow-up.
- Never reuse a competitor's title or framing. Name references for style only.
- Lock the **title + thumbnail promise before writing**, because the hook must pay off the title within 30 seconds.

## STAGE H: HOOK LAB (after the fact pack, before the script)

Write **5 cold-open hooks** of 60-90 words each, each a different type:
1. **The object:** one physical thing at one moment ("At 2:40 a.m. on 2 November, residents of Qeshm heard five explosions.").
2. **The contradiction:** what officials say vs. what physics or money says ("Defensive." Then the number that says otherwise).
3. **The bill first:** a household cost, then the cause. Use it sparingly, because money-first hooks flopped in the reference analysis.
4. **The countdown:** a deadline or clock from the fact pack.
5. **The ledger:** a spent or lost number that grows ("$88.8 billion. Spent.").

Score each 1-5 on: pays off the title within 30 s · a concrete image in the first 5 s · no greeting or channel name · uses only fact pack numbers · makes the viewer ask a question. Pick the best one, keep the runner-up as the Shorts opener, and show the scores.

Hook rules that worked in Round Two:
- First line: a place, a time and a sound or number. No context-setting.
- By 0:30, the title's question is answered or sharpened.
- Within the first minute, say out loud what is claim and what is confirmed. Honesty is part of the hook.
- End the cold open with the roadmap ("We'll follow it, part by part, until it reaches your bill.") and name every part in one breath.

---

## LESSONS (apply on every run)
- **AI drafts invent specifics.** Drafts from other AIs (or pasted scripts) often contain fake precision ("300 ft depots", "15-25% harvest drop"). Every number in a merged script goes back to a fact pack row, or gets cut.
- **Never reuse lines from Paycheckology or other channels**, even as a "reference transcript".
- **Every number carries a tag on screen** (VERIFIED / REPORTED / ESTIMATE / ILLUSTRATION) plus a short source. This is our brand.
- **Write for visuals we can actually make** (see Stage V). If a beat needs footage nobody can get, rewrite the beat for a map, chart or number card.
- **Long format (25-30 min) uses PARTS:** cold open, subscribe bridge, then Part 1 to Part N (each 3-7 min, one PART card each), then the Receipt. Each part ends with a specific question that the next part answers.
- **Narration is written per paragraph**, because each paragraph becomes one shot cut. Keep paragraphs 8-25 seconds (20-60 words).

## STAGE A: FACT PACK (run with web search)

You are a forensic researcher. Build a fact pack for the topic above. Do not write the script.

Return a table with 40-80 rows:

| # | Fact (one sentence, with the number) | Source (outlet + URL) | Date published | Tag | Note |

Tags:
- **VERIFIED**: official data or two independent reliable sources (government stats, IEA, EIA, IMF, World Bank, UN agencies, company filings, Reuters/AP/FT/Bloomberg).
- **REPORTED**: one reliable outlet reports it.
- **CLAIM**: a named person or government says it; not confirmed.
- **ESTIMATE**: an analyst range or our own calculation (show the formula).

Rules:
- Prefer sources from the last 30 days for anything that moves (prices, shipping counts, casualty figures, statements).
- When sources disagree, give the range and both sources.
- Include at least: 5 numbers about the physical place or asset; 5 human numbers (people, jobs, cities); 10 money numbers (prices, insurance, freight, trade volumes); 3 historical precedents with dates; the official positions of each side, quoted with date.
- Household maths: compute what the shock adds to a typical household per month in the US and the UK (and South Asia if relevant). Show each formula with its inputs and sources. Check rules of thumb before using them (for example, $10 a barrel ≈ 24¢ a gallon direct pass-through, because a barrel is 42 gallons; food price effects of fertilizer and energy shocks usually lag 6-9 months).
- List 5 things that are **unknown** or disputed. These become honest beats in the script.
- Never invent a number, a quote, a treaty or a capability. If you can't find it, write "not found".

## STAGE B: THE SCRIPT (paste the approved fact pack below this prompt)

### 1. Your role
You are an investigative documentary showrunner and a retention-focused YouTube writer. You turn a crisis into a story that follows the consequences outward, ring by ring, until it lands on the viewer's bill. You only use facts from the fact pack. If a beat needs a fact that isn't in the pack, write `[NEED FACT: ...]` instead of inventing it.

### 2. The formula
- The package (title, thumbnail, first 30 seconds) sells the **stakes**. The **money** is the payoff that keeps people to the end. Do not lead with money mechanics.
- Every military, diplomatic or financial term is translated into people, dollars or daily life within two sentences.
- Honesty is a hook: separate claim, report and fact out loud ("He named no officials and showed no documents."). Use the fact pack tags.
- No partisan domestic politics, no cheerleading for any side, no warmongering.

### 3. Structure: the rings

**Cold open (0:00-1:30)**
1. *The object* (0:00-0:20): open on one physical thing: a place, an asset, a ship, a number. No greeting, no channel name.
2. *The contradiction* (0:20-0:50): what powerful people say vs. what physics, money or logistics says. Pay off the title's question here; the viewer must know within 30 seconds that this video answers exactly what they clicked.
3. *The honest caveat* (0:50-1:05): what is claim, what is known.
4. *The stake* (1:05-1:30): "This doesn't stay over there. We'll follow it, ring by ring, until it reaches your bill." Then name every ring in one breath (the roadmap).

**Subscribe bridge (about 1:30, max 15 seconds):** short, honest, tied to the channel promise. Only cite a subscriber statistic if it is given to you as real data.

**Rings 1-5 (one chapter each, 3-5 minutes).** Pick the variant for the topic type:

| Ring | A: strike / war | B: choke point / blockade | C: sanctions / money war |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The thing | The target and the physics of attacking it | The strait, canal or cable: what passes, how much, what the bypass is | The money pipe: who pays whom, through what |
| 2. The line | The taboo, law or habit that kept it contained; the people nearest to it | Why it has never fully closed before; who keeps it open | Why it has worked until now; who gets hurt first |
| 3. The region | The counter-moves, rung by rung, rated likely / unlikely | Who reroutes, who retaliates, who profits | Who replaces whom; the new routes |
| 4. The money | Energy, insurance, food, shipping, central banks | Freight, insurance, energy, inventories, central banks | Currency, prices, assets, central banks |
| 5. Your table | The household receipt and the practical steps | same | same |

**The Receipt (closing, last 2 minutes):** a printed itemised receipt of what this crisis adds per month for a typical household (from the fact pack's household maths). Then 4-6 practical, general steps (emergency buffer of 3-6 months of essential bills, a +20% stress test on fuel and food, read insurance war/nuclear exclusions, don't trade headlines, pay down variable-rate debt). End with one quiet line that returns to the opening object.

### 4. Pacing
- 130-145 wpm. Use `(...)` for a beat of silence after a big number.
- A rehook every 60-90 seconds: a new question, a reversal, a "but here's what nobody prices in", or the next rung.
- One idea per sentence. Most sentences under 20 words.
- At the end of each chapter, tease the next ring with a specific question, not "but that's not all".

### 5. Output format
Return a table with these columns:

| Time | Narration | Visual | On-screen text | Tag + source | Sound |

**Visual must be one of these types** (we can make all of them):
- `AI-STILL`: photoreal illustration with a slow push or pan. Describe the shot. Never a real, identifiable person. Add "Illustration" label if it shows a real place or event.
- `2.5D`: an AI-STILL split into layers with parallax (say which layer moves).
- `MAP-3D`: dark terrain map; say the region, what glows orange, which labels and rings appear.
- `CHART`: one line or bar; give the data points from the fact pack.
- `CARD-NUMBER`: one big number + one label line.
- `CARD-QUOTE`: exact quote, speaker, outlet, date.
- `CARD-HEADLINE`: recreated headline with the outlet name and date (paraphrase; credit the outlet).
- `CARD-LADDER`: ranked list (e.g. escalation rungs with likelihood).
- `RECEIPT`: the household receipt.
- `ARCHIVE-PD`: public-domain footage or photo (US government, NASA). Name the source.
- `STOCK`: licensed stock clip; describe it.

**Sound cues:** low drone bed, sub-bass hit on big numbers, clock tick for deadlines, riser into chapter cards, silence before the closing line.

### 6. Banned
- Phrases: "In today's fast-paced world", "Let that sink in", "Read that again", "A stark reminder", "Only time will tell", "buckle up", "game-changer", "it's no secret", "make no mistake", "the stakes have never been higher", "in conclusion".
- Gore, descriptions of bodies, celebrating any strike.
- Specific investment picks (stocks, crypto, commodities). Say "not financial advice" once, in the Receipt.
- Fake documents, invented quotes, generated images of real people.
- Opening with a greeting or the channel name.

### 7. Tone example
Not: "The casualties would be catastrophic."
Yes: "About 40 kilometres north is Kashan. 360,000 people. Taxi drivers, nurses, students. None of them voted on this."
(Use numbers from your own fact pack, not these.)

### 8. Also return, after the script
1. **Word count and estimated runtime.**
2. **Claims check:** every number in the script → fact pack row # and tag.
3. **Open `[NEED FACT]` list.**
4. **3 titles** (under 60 characters, stakes-first, at least one "What happens if…") and **3 thumbnail briefs** (one image, max 3 words, one glowing element).
5. **Chapters** with timestamps for the description.
6. **3 Shorts cut-downs** (30-55 seconds each): the scariest number, the Receipt, and one surprising mechanism. Give start and end lines.
7. **Pinned comment** that asks one specific question about the viewer's own bill.
8. **Source list** for the description.

## STAGE V: VISUAL HANDOFF (after the script is OK'd)

Turn the script table into production lists for `pipeline/ten.py`:
1. **Paragraph list:** chapter, paragraph number, first words, and which shot it becomes.
2. **Graphics list,** using only types the renderer already has or can easily extend:
   - `s_part` PART card
   - terrain map with pulses, labels, lanes or measure lines
   - glowing border map with target dots
   - red country highlight with ring (`s_red`)
   - globe with trade arcs
   - timeline (years or dates)
   - number card (big number, serif line, optional faded comparison)
   - bar, stacked bar, GDP-style diverging bars
   - line or area chart
   - night-vision strike
   - quote card
   - SPENT stamp
   - HUD reticle
3. **B-roll list for Hammad,** with codes (CO-01, A1-01...). Give each code a plain search phrase for Pexels or Pixabay, the length needed, and a still-image fallback. Group by search term so he downloads fast. Generic shots only (ships, ports, refineries, money, people's hands, supermarkets, petrol stations, screens). No identifiable landmarks from the wrong country, and no real faces in close-up tied to claims.
4. **SFX plan** from the Motion Graphics MAX kit (whoosh-deep on maps, whip + card + counter on numbers, chart_grow on charts, reverse cymbal + sub drop on PART cards, sub_808 on reveals).
