## 2. Hook
- The first 15 s are a promise ("high-end documentary people actually want to watch") plus a shortcut ("steal my prompt"). Then the proof plays at 0:15 with no title card.
- **The real hook is the demo itself.** Dates as hard anchors ("1950.", "1956.", "1958.") with a hit on each, then a reversal ("Then it fails.") at 0:34. A date + a turn inside 20 seconds.

## 3. The two demo styles, designed
**Demo 1, "dark data stream"** (the one commenters preferred):
- One continuous 2.5D camera move through a single connected space. No hard cuts in 40 s.
- Beat length 3.5-5 s per idea. It feels calm because the camera never stops; it feels fast because something new arrives every 4 s.
- Palette: near-black slate `#0d1114`, bone white, one red accent `#ff3b30`. Only one accent colour.
- Every beat = a **large serif date** + a real photo card with viewfinder corners + a document, joined by a red line to the next beat.
- Hand-drawn red ovals circle the key words on real documents ("Can machines think?", "walk, talk", "conscious").
- A red distressed **FAILED** stamp slams on the newspaper headline.
- The "dark winter" beat literally dims the screen before the next idea lights up.
- Ends on data: monospace counters climbing (EXAMPLES, ADJUSTMENTS), a network diagram, a curve rising to a red target, resolving into a halftone dot portrait.
- Texture: fine grain, vignette, faint scanlines, HUD reticles, a timeline track along the bottom.

**Demo 2, "corkboard evidence wall"**: the same beats as physical objects (cork, pins, red string, manila cards, CASE FILE tags, a flashlight beam finding "wanted" silhouettes in the dark section). Warmer, more tactile, slightly busier.

## 4. Sound
- Sound is what the audience noticed most: the top 2 comments (93 and 47 likes) and many more ask only for his SFX pack.
- Every hit is a **physical object sound**, matched to what is on screen: paper rustle, pen-circle scribble, stamp slam, typewriter, pin click, string twang, flashlight click. Under it: low rumble + sub hit on each date, a riser into the last beat, one clean bass hit to end.
- He used AI for a first sound pass, then fixed SFX by hand. The hand pass is the quality.

## 5. Packaging

- **Thumbnail:** host face (left), laptop showing the result (a redacted mugshot dossier with the red word "Liars"), a robot editing timelines, and the Claude logo with an arrow. The result is the hero object; the dossier look reads instantly at small size.

## 6. His own lesson, which matches ours
His drafts went: v1 "looked like a PowerPoint" → v2 he asked Claude for **a list of every asset it needed** and handed over real photos → v3 he listed exactly what was wrong: too fast to read, the same phrase popping up in several places, rough camera. He told it to slow down, spend more time on design, smooth the camera, prioritise readability, and **ask questions when unclear**. Once the style was locked, the next ones were fast.

## 7. Takeaways for The Invoice (our own versions, not copies)
1. **The Money Trail one-take.** Our original version of his continuous move: one giant canvas where the camera travels the chain from cause to bill (strait → tanker → refinery → wholesale price → pump → your receipt), with an **orange thread** (our accent, already locked) linking each station. It ends on a printed receipt line. It is the channel's idea ("ring by ring to your bill") turned into camera grammar, and it can become our signature open.
2. **Date + price anchors.** Each beat of a cold open lands on a big serif anchor with a hit. His anchors are years; ours are **date + price** (e.g. "MARCH 3 · $97 A BARREL"). It gives viewers a timeline they can feel.
3. **Mark the evidence.** On recreated headlines and official documents (already in our evidence-first list), animate an orange marker circle or underline around the 2-4 words that matter, timed to the narrator saying them. Cheap to build, and it tells the eye where to read.
4. **"Lights out" beat for the bad news.** When the story turns (a strait closes, a reserve runs out), dim the whole frame for a second before the next element lights up. A visual version of our `(...)` pause.
5. **A Follow-the-Money board for "who profits" sections.** His corkboard style, made ours: a dark ink-black board with paper cards, orange thread and Plex Mono tags (VERIFIED / REPORTED). Only for investigative chapters, so it stays special.
6. **Object foley on every graphic hit.** Paper slide when a document appears, marker squeak on the circle, stamp slam on SPENT, typewriter on the source badge, a till "ka-ching" or receipt-printer chatter on the receipt. Layer them over the kit whooshes and impacts we already use.
7. **Same audio, two skins.** Before locking the look for a new series, render the same 30 s of narration in two styles and let Hammad pick. Our Round Two look test was one skin; a second skin costs about an hour.
8. **Packaging test pattern.** Try a bracket ending in our titles: stakes, then a personal hook, e.g. "What Happens If Hormuz Closes for a Year (Check Your Bill)". The thumbnail lesson: one evidence object (a receipt, a stamped document, a map with a red ring) beats a busy scene.
9. **Pace numbers.** One-take sections can run 3.5-5 s per beat because the moving camera keeps it calm. Body sections stay at one change every 6-10 s.

## 8. Proposed additions to the standing process (add on top, nothing replaced)
For `video-process.md` / `pipeline/PROCESS.md`, under a new heading "Upgrades from Reference 01 (2026-11-04)":

- **Step 0b, reference board.** For each new video (or each new look), save 12-20 reference frames in 3 lanes (3D documentary, newspaper/document animation, map animation) to `Documentry\refs\INV-NNN\`. Claude writes a 10-line style spec from them before the storyboard. Pinterest stays inspiration only.
- **Step 4b, one-take open.** The cold open (and optionally each PART opener) is built as one continuous camera move across a single large canvas (new graphic **G27 `g_world`**: a ~8000×4500 canvas with all elements placed, camera keyframes with ease-in-out, cropped per frame). The cut-based shot list stays for the body.
- **Step 5b, asset request list.** Before building, Claude lists every real item a scene needs (public-domain photos, official documents, headlines to recreate, AIS captures) so drafts never start from empty shapes.
- **Step 8b, three extra checks:**
  - *Readability:* each on-screen text stays up for at least 1 s + 0.3 s per word (minimum 2 s).
  - *No repeats:* the same phrase never appears on screen twice in one part unless it is the hero callback.
  - *Camera:* no linear moves; every move eases in and out, and snaps happen only on hits.
- **Step 9, object foley layer.** Map each graphic type to an object sound (paper, marker, stamp, typewriter, receipt printer) from the kit, layered on top of the existing hit stack. If the kit lacks one, list it for Hammad to download. Then a manual pass: listen and move any SFX that lands off the frame.
- **Feedback rule.** When a draft looks like a slideshow, fix in this order: real assets → slower beats → camera depth and parallax → remove repeated text.
